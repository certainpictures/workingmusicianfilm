Synopsis

Working Musician: Bach Plays the Berkshires is a short film about the evolving significance of J.S. Bach’s music in today’s culture, told through the lens of the Berkshire Bach Society.

The film features interviews with pianist Simone Dinnerstein, BBS Artistic Director and violinist Eugene Drucker, composer Bruce Adolphe, and critic Tim Page on what makes Bach’s music important for modern listeners. It includes revealing anecdotes from Bach’s life and follows the preparations for Berkshire Bach’s signature series Bach at New Year’s featuring the six Brandenburg Concertos.

Moving between rehearsals, performances, backstage spaces, and animation, the film reveals the curiosity, negotiation, and discovery behind music that can appear fixed on the page but changes each time musicians come together to play it.

The piece gives the organization a way to communicate its artistic identity beyond a conventional institutional profile. It can support audience development, fundraising, partnerships, presentations, and public relations while standing on its own as a film about music, creativity, collaboration, and the endurance of cultural work.
